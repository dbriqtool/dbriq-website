Why I built dbriq

I’ve been a database administrator for twelve years, looking after SQL Server and Azure SQL databases that can’t go down. Over that time the estates grew: more servers, more cloud, more teams depending on them. The day-to-day monitoring didn’t keep up.

The hard part was rarely the fix. It was finding the problem. An alert would say CPU was high or a job had failed, and then the real work began: which query, which session, what changed, and what is the safe thing to do about it. That meant jumping between tools, running my own scripts, and piecing the answer together under pressure.

dbriq is the tool I wanted on every one of those days. It watches the whole estate, and when something goes wrong it says in plain words what the bottleneck is, which query or session is behind it, why it is slow, what changed shortly before, and the script that fixes it. It never runs that script for you. That decision stays with the DBA.

dbriq is in beta now, and the people using it shape what comes next. If something doesn’t work the way you would expect on your servers, I want to hear about it.

Vinay Yegineni, founder

How dbriq is built

Your servers stay yours

dbriq runs on your own PC or server and connects with a read-only login. Nothing it collects is sent to us.

Advice, not autopilot

Every finding comes with a fix script to review. dbriq never changes anything on your databases.

Plain words first

A finding should say what is wrong and what to do, the way an experienced DBA would explain it to a colleague.

Light on production

Monitoring queries are read-only with short timeouts, and reports run on short connections with a low deadlock priority.
